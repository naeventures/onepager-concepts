General arrangement

Ostwyn Industrial Group

A holding company for machining, fabrication and industrial electronics businesses in the north of England, bought to be kept and run with no exit date to work against.

The group

01

Ostwyn is a holding company and not a fund. There is no timetable to sell against, and that changes the first question asked about a business: not what it would fetch on resale, but whether it can be run better and kept.

02

The group works only in trades it can read: machining, fabrication, industrial electronics, and the service businesses attached to them. Where there is no operating knowledge there is no bid, whatever the price looks like on paper.

03

It does not restructure distressed companies, does not break a business up for its parts, and does not take a minority position it cannot be useful inside. An owner is told which of those a conversation is heading towards before anyone spends a day on it.

The founder

IO

Drawn byInes Ostwyn

RoleFounder

Pronounsshe, her

Ines Ostwyn trained as a mechanical engineer and spent the first years of her career on the floor of a machine tool works, ending up in charge of its service arm. She left to take on a family fabrication business whose succession had run out, and stayed nine years. That is where Ostwyn comes from: the business did not need a strategy, it needed someone to keep the decisions that were already right and stop repeating the ones that were not.

Ostwyn exists to do that on purpose rather than by accident. Her own work is deliberately narrow. She reads the operation, spends time with the people who run it, and signs off the short list of decisions a new owner should make in the first year, which is usually shorter than a seller expects. The rest stays with the managers who were already getting it right.

She answers enquiries herself.

Focus areas

01

Machining and precision components

Subcontract machinists with real process knowledge and tired planning behind it.

Out of scope Volume work that only pays at a scale the shop cannot reach.

02

Fabrication and structures

Metalwork businesses whose reputation sits with two or three people near the end of their careers.

Out of scope Anything that only works if the workforce is halved.

03

Industrial electronics and controls

Panel builders and repair houses attached to machinery that will outlive them, with service revenue never treated as the product.

Out of scope Software led automation with no service arm behind it.

04

Service and aftermarket

Maintenance, calibration and spares businesses that came second to a parent company’s main line.

Out of scope Distress, receivership and turnaround work.
